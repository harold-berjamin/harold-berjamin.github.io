Composite laminates can be designed to reach desired engineering properties such as low weight and high stiffness. This study concerns periodic media made of alternating layers of soft rubber-like solids. Assuming incompressible and perfectly bonded elastic layers, the effective mechanical response of the laminate is estimated by means of homogenisation theory. This involves computing the spatial average of the energy stored in the layers, each of which is in equilibrium and homogeneously deformed. The study is conducted for layers whose elastic energy depends on two scalar invariants, in general. Beyond neo-Hookean solids, explicit analytical expressions are obtained for a special class of Mooney–Rivlin materials, whereas approximate expressions are obtained for Mooney–Rivlin laminates and for Yeoh laminates. These stratified media can be represented by a transversely isotropic elasticity theory at the macroscopic scale. Based on these results, an approximate effective energy for laminates whose layers combine Gent-type and Mooney–Rivlin behaviour is postulated. It is then used to estimate the macroscopic instability threshold under pure shear numerically. Results show that the specific mechanical response of individual layers has negligible influence on the macroscopic stability limit if a certain proportionality relationship between their energy functions holds. Furthermore, in a pre-stressed layered medium under pure shear, the influence of the individual mechanical response of the layers on the shear wave velocity is found to be comparable to that observed in homogeneous media, within the same range of investigated situations. Finally, more significant effects may be observed in non-proportional laminates.
